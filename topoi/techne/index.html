T12 / TOPOI

An Ereignis reading path

Know how. Ask what the knowing reveals.

What changes when technē is read as a way of revealing rather than simply a means of making?

Follow Heidegger’s interpretation of technē through knowing, bringing-forth, and the question of modern technology. Read the Greek inheritance as an argument with a history, not as an uncontested dictionary definition.

01 / Archive trail

Knowing and the reach of technics.

Both stops come from The Question Concerning Technology. The first uses the archive’s parallel-pagination reading edition; the second links to a specific page in Basic Writings.

  1. 01 / The Question Concerning Technology · GA 7

    Technē and knowing

    In the passage marked Basic Writings p. 319, Heidegger places technē with knowing and revealing. Making a house or a vessel involves a prior gathering of what is to appear. Its significance exceeds manipulation alone.

    Read the essay / locate the p. 319 marker
  2. 02 / The Question Concerning Technology · GA 7

    Art reopens the question

    Near the essay’s end, Heidegger recalls art under the name technē while asking about the danger that ordering will exhaust revealing. The passage raises a possibility; it does not certify every artwork as a cure.

    Basic Writings, p. 339

02 / Terms and distinctions

Knowing, making, ordering.

TermDistinction
Technē / τέχνηHeidegger’s reading emphasizes a knowing that reveals. “Craft,” “art,” and “technique” each illuminate only part of the problem.
Poiēsis / ποίησιςBringing-forth: follow the neighboring Topos to distinguish this from sheer production volume.
Technik / modern technologyDo not simply substitute the modern category for the Greek word. The essay argues for a distinctive mode of revealing.
Gestell / enframingThe ordering mode explored in the standing-reserve Topos. A diagnosis of revealing requires more than an inventory of machines.

03 / Readers at Ereignis

Two routes through the historical shift.

EQUIPMENT AND HISTORY

Hubert Dreyfus

“Between Techne and Technology” provocatively reads Being and Time’s account of equipment as a decisive step toward the later technological understanding. This complicates an easy opposition between good craft and bad machines.

Read at Ereignis
WORLD AND ART

William McNeill

“Tracing Technē” follows production, world-formation, and the question of art across Heidegger’s changing engagement with Aristotle. His account draws attention to a disclosure of world that cannot simply be credited to a maker’s plan.

Read at Ereignis

04 / Disagreement and risk

Does craft escape the problem?

Dreyfus and McNeill trace different emphases within a changing corpus. Compare their arguments before treating “Heidegger on technē” as one stable position. Neither a handmade object nor a digital tool decides the question in advance.

Ask what a practice lets its participants recognize, what counts as success, and what it hides. Regional grammars help compare these operations within particular settings; they do not assign one timeless technical mentality to an entire culture.

05 / Reading move

Read one act of making through its knowing.

Experimental A BeyngLab exercise. Keep your extension distinct from the source.

  1. 01

    Name the work.

    Choose a repair or making task. Record the materials, anticipated outcome, and skills involved.

  2. 02

    Notice the revealing.

    What becomes visible only to someone learning this practice? What does the task train them to disregard? Return to the p. 319 passage.

  3. 03

    Compare without ranking in advance.

    Try a different tool or procedure. Describe what changes in attention and judgment, not just speed. Use Dreyfus or McNeill to challenge your initial account.
