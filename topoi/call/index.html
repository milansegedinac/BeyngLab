T5 / TOPOI

An Ereignis reading path

Who calls? Who can answer?

How can a call come from me without being something I choose to say?

Begin with the call of conscience in Being and Time. Follow its unsettling relation to selfhood and care, then ask what kind of response it makes possible. Keep this analysis distinct from later accounts of the call of being.

01 / Archive trail

An address without an outside speaker.

Two consecutive pages in §57 let us follow the argument without importing a theory of revelation or an inner moral adviser. Read the surrounding section as well as these entry points.

  1. 01 / Being and Time §57

    Neither planned nor delivered by someone else

    The call interrupts expectation and volition. Heidegger refuses both an immediate appeal to a divine speaker and a reduction to an objectively present mechanism. The problem concerns the way Dasein is, rather than the identification of an unseen entity.

    Macquarrie–Robinson, p. 320
  2. 02 / Being and Time §57

    The caller in uncanniness

    Heidegger locates the caller in Dasein’s not-being-at-home: its thrown existence becomes exposed as familiar assurances recede. This helps explain why what addresses me need not feel like a familiar account of who I am.

    Macquarrie–Robinson, p. 321

02 / Terms and distinctions

Address, conscience, uncanniness.

TermReading distinction
Ruf / callTrack who calls, who is addressed, and what changes in the address. Do not assume a message transmitted between two separate people.
Gewissen / conscienceThe existential analysis needs to be distinguished from a list of moral instructions. Ask how responsibility is opened rather than assuming the call supplies a rule.
Unheimlichkeit / uncanninessNot-being-at-home names a disturbance of familiar self-understanding. It is not the name of a cultural identity.
Call of beingA later vocabulary with its own textual setting. A resemblance of words does not establish an unchanged argument.

03 / Readers at Ereignis

Care, method, and a later crossing.

WISEACRE / CARE AND NULLITY

Doug Wise

“The importance of the nothingness of what we care about” connects conscience, care, and nullity through Heidegger, Pippin, and Crowell. Wise’s evolutionary comparison and provocative description of the call are extensions to examine, not substitutes for §57.

Read Wise at Ereignis
METHOD AND EXPERIENCE

Katherine Withy

“The Methodological Role of Angst in Being and Time” questions the assumption that anxiety’s philosophical role is exhausted by an existential story about authenticity. Use this reading to test the movement from uncanniness to a claim about how to live.

Read Withy on angst
A DIFFERENT TEXTUAL SETTING

Withy on Antigone

“Authenticity and Heidegger’s Antigone” reads responsiveness and reticence in the 1942 Ister lectures. This is a route into a later call of being, not evidence that conscience in 1927 and Antigone in 1942 mean the same thing.

04 / Disagreement and risk

What makes an address answerable?

BeyngLab places these readings together as a question, not as a documented debate between their authors. Does the call disclose responsibility, and what would connect that disclosure to reasons others can contest?

An intense feeling of being summoned does not establish that a proposed action is justified. Equally, interpreting the caller as Dasein does not make it a self-command that can be produced at will. Preserve both difficulties when moving from the text to an example.

05 / Reading move

Separate the interruption from your explanation.

Experimental A BeyngLab reading exercise.

  1. 01

    Map the address.

    Read pp. 320–321. Note what the text says about the caller, the one addressed, and the absence of a planned message.

  2. 02

    Describe a modest interruption.

    Choose a situation in which an accepted role became questionable. Separate what happened from the story you later gave it. This is a comparison to test, not proof that you experienced Heidegger’s call.

  3. 03

    Make the response contestable.

    Write one possible response and one objection from someone affected. Keep the objection beside the response rather than treating the call as its authorization.
