T1 / TOPOI

An Ereignis reading path / Being and Time

Already involved. Open to possibility.

What does Heidegger mean when he calls Dasein’s being “care”?

Read Sorge through its relation to world, possibility, and being with others. Follow the primary passages at beyng.com, then ask how their formal account meets bodily need, dependence, and assistance.

01 / Primary passages

Read the structure and its differences.

The page references below distinguish the German standard pagination (SZ), the collected edition (GA 2), and each English edition. Matching page numbers across editions do not necessarily name the same passage.

  1. 01 / §41 · GA 2: 255 · SZ 192

    Care as a whole

    Dasein projects possibilities from a situation it already inhabits while engaging with what it encounters. The care structure gathers these dimensions; it does not name one feeling or activity alongside others.

    Macquarrie–Robinson, p. 236Continue on p. 237German SZ 192
  2. 02 / §26 · SZ 121–122

    Concern and solicitude

    Heidegger distinguishes dealings with things from being with others. Solicitude can take over another’s concern or help the other recover their own possibilities. The passage also names indifferent and deficient ways of being with others.

    M&R p. 157 / translation notep. 158 / leaping inp. 159 / leaping ahead
  3. 03 / §42 · SZ 199–200

    Care and ordinary cares

    The ontological claim concerns the constitution that makes particular cares and forms of dedication possible. It does not generalize from the observation that people often worry. This distinction also limits what one can infer about an ethics of assistance.

    Stambaugh–Schmidt, p. 192

02 / Translation matters

Sorge, Besorgen, Fürsorge.

GermanM&R renderingReading distinction
SorgeCareThe unified structure of Dasein’s being. Kindness and anxiety do not exhaust its meaning.
BesorgenConcernWays of dealing with what is encountered within the world.
FürsorgeSolicitudeWays of being with others, including taking over, enabling, and indifference.

Macquarrie and Robinson flag the loss of the German words’ visible kinship in English at p. 157. Read their note before treating these as three unrelated categories.

03 / Distinct readings at Ereignis

Meaning, embodiment, praxis.

MEANING

Thomas Sheehan

In “Heidegger’s Doctrine of Meaning,” the section on Sorge argues for reading it as the existential structure of engagement in meaning. His strong objection to psychological translations gives the familiar English word “care” a problem to answer.

Read Sheehan at Ereignis
BODY AND NEED

Babette Babich

“Da-Sein’s Body” brings Günther Anders’ criticism of Heidegger into view: how much does an ostensibly concrete analytic disclose about bodily need and dependence? This challenges the account’s reach without turning Sorge into a synonym for nursing.

Read Babich at Ereignis
ARISTOTLE AND PRAXIS

Franco Volpi

“Dasein as Praxis” reads care through Heidegger’s transformation of Aristotelian practical philosophy. Ask what this genealogy changes in an account centered on meaning or selfhood.

Read Volpi at Ereignis

04 / Interpretive problem

What does the formal account leave open?

Sheehan’s reading guards against collapsing an existential structure into a psychological state. Babich’s encounter with Anders presses a different question: can the analytic adequately account for embodied and dependent lives? These emphases form a BeyngLab comparison, not a documented exchange between the authors.

Return to §26 before deciding that the text simply neglects others. Return to bodily need before deciding that an ontological distinction settles their claims. Neither the care structure nor the contrast between leaping in and leaping ahead supplies a complete rule for every act of assistance.

RiskCorrection
Care means kindnessDistinguish Sorge from particular caring actions, and read the indifferent modes of Fürsorge.
Authenticity means self-sufficiencyKeep being-with, thrownness, and dependence in the reading.
The helper already knowsAsk whether assistance displaces another’s possibilities and who can contest the helper’s description.

05 / Reading move

Keep three claims apart.

  1. 01

    Mark the primary claim.

    Read §41 beside §26. Record what each says about care, concern, and solicitude, with the edition and page.

  2. 02

    Choose an interpretation.

    Read Sheehan, Babich, or Volpi. State one argument of the author’s and the passage it changes for you.

  3. 03

    Form your own question.

    Ask how assistance can support or displace another’s possibilities. Mark that extension as yours, and keep an objection beside it.
