An Ereignis reading path

Identity and difference. What comes first?

Must difference begin with identities—or can identity emerge through difference?

Begin with Identity and Difference: Heidegger’s thinking of belonging-together and his encounter with Hegel. Follow distinct crossings into Derrida’s différance and Deleuze’s difference in itself, while keeping the earlier ontological question and other sources of twentieth-century difference in view.

01 / Archive trail

Identity and Difference: the principal reading.

The 1957 book brings together two lectures, not simply one commentary on Hegel. Read “The Principle of Identity” with “The Onto-theo-logical Constitution of Metaphysics.” The second makes the encounter with Hegel explicit. English page references below use Joan Stambaugh’s translation.

  1. 01 / Identity and Difference · GA 11

    Identity as belonging-together

    The first lecture moves toward the belonging-together of thinking and being, and toward Ereignis, translated here as appropriation. Identity becomes a question about this relation rather than merely a formula of self-sameness.

    The Principle of Identity, p. 39
  2. 02 / Identity and Difference · Hegel

    What is the matter of thinking?

    Heidegger reads Hegel’s absolute Idea and asks how being becomes the matter of thought. Read this as Heidegger’s interpretation of Hegel, not a neutral summary that settles Hegel’s philosophy.

    The Onto-theo-logical Constitution of Metaphysics, p. 43
  3. 03 / Identity and Difference · historical encounter

    How should thinking meet its history?

    Heidegger considers Hegel’s criterion for dialogue with earlier thinkers. Compare taking up the force of what they thought with questioning what their thinking leaves unthought.

    Hegel and the conversation with history, p. 47
  4. 04 / Identity and Difference · the step back

    Being thought through difference

    The step back asks about the difference that metaphysics presupposes. It changes the question asked of metaphysics rather than simply adding another highest ground.

    Difference and the step back, p. 65

Earlier bearings: keep the distinction between being and beings separate from differences among ways of being.

  1. 01 / Introduction to Metaphysics · GA 40

    The ambiguity of “being”

    Heidegger distinguishes a particular thing from what makes up its being. The passage moves through the ambiguity of the Greek expression to on. Read the translators’ notes as notes, alongside the argument.

    Fried–Polt translation, second edition, p. 34
  2. 02 / The Basic Problems of Phenomenology · GA 24

    Many ways, one question?

    The lecture asks how radically different ways of being can belong to any unified concept of being. This concerns the relation among ways of being, rather than merely the separation of two classes of objects.

    Hofstadter translation, p. 176

02 / Terms and distinctions

Related words, different operations.

TermDistinction
Identity / belonging-togetherIn the 1957 lecture, ask what lets thinking and being belong together. Do not translate this immediately into personal or cultural identity.
Hegel / dialectical differenceFollow the roles of identity, negation, and mediation in the argument. Hegel is an interlocutor, not a shorthand for denying all difference.
Derrida / différanceDiffering and deferring unsettle self-presence. This is not another name for the distinction between being and beings.
Deleuze / difference in itselfAsk how difference can be thought without first subordinating it to identity, resemblance, opposition, or analogy.
Seiendes / beingsThe things, people, and other entities encountered. Distinguishing one entity from another does not yet pose the ontological question.
Sein / beingThe question of the being of what appears. Avoid treating it as an additional entity behind the others.
Ways of beingRead differences among existence, readiness-to-hand, and presence-at-hand without turning them into a ranking of human cultures.
Differenz / UnterschiedAttend to the work and passage in which a term occurs. Later uses of difference need their own argument; English “difference” does not guarantee conceptual identity.

03 / Readers at Ereignis

Derrida and Deleuze: engagement and departure.

EXTERNAL PRIMARY TEXT / DERRIDA

Différance

Derrida explicitly engages Heidegger’s ontological difference while asking whether différance can remain within its terms. His essay’s encounter with Heidegger centers on “The Anaximander Fragment,” so the relation should not be reduced to influence from Identity and Difference alone. His discussion of Hegel through Koyré also complicates any simple anti-Hegelian label.

Différance (1968), trans. Alan Bass, Margins of Philosophy, pp. 3–27
EXTERNAL PRIMARY TEXT / DELEUZE

Difference and Repetition

The original preface names Heidegger’s ontological difference alongside structuralism, literature, and other contemporary experiments. Deleuze seeks difference in itself rather than difference governed by identity and negation. His “Note on Heidegger’s Philosophy of Difference” (Patton translation, pp. 64–66) makes the engagement explicit; read it as an argument with Heidegger, not an adoption of his project.

Read the prefaces to Difference and Repetition

Further readers at Ereignis

WAYS OF SAYING

Lee Braver

“How to say the same thing” distinguishes entities, their ways of being, and manifestation itself. His interpretive scheme helps expose why treating every distinction as one between things misses the problem. Keep the scheme marked as Braver’s reading.

Read at Ereignis
WISEACRE / A LIMIT QUESTION

Doug Wise

“Topology of being and its limit theorem” asks what would make a distinction’s two sides and their relation intelligible. Wise questions the possibility of stepping outside the horizon of sense-making to survey its setting. His topological vocabulary is an interpretive experiment.

Read at Ereignis

04 / Disagreement and risk

Did Heidegger introduce difference to the twentieth century?

A decisive intervention, not a single origin. Heidegger gives ontological difference a central role and later rethinks identity through belonging-together. But the field has multiple sources: Nietzsche, Bergson, Saussure and structuralism, Freud, and the inheritance of Hegel. Deleuze’s preface itself presents a convergence rather than one founder; Derrida’s essay works through several of these sources.

Keep three claims separate: a direct citation establishes engagement; a shared problem supports comparison; a stronger claim of influence needs a history of reading and transformation. These pages establish explicit engagements with Heidegger, not that Identity and Difference alone generated either later project.

Can a useful diagram become a misleading picture?

Braver’s distinctions clarify the language of manifestation; Wise asks about the limits of any position from which we might survey such distinctions. Their juxtaposition here is BeyngLab’s question, not a claim that these essays directly debate each other.

Two boxes labeled “being” and “beings” can make them look like two things. Ask what your diagram clarifies and what its spatial arrangement silently assumes. Likewise, differences among regional grammars concern situated ways of disclosure, not separate cultural substances with permanent properties.

05 / Reading move

Read a crossing without erasing the difference.

Experimental A BeyngLab exercise. Keep your extension distinct from the source.

  1. 01

    Read Heidegger with Hegel.

    Begin with Identity and Difference, pp. 39, 43, 47, and 65. Write separately what Heidegger attributes to Hegel and what Heidegger proposes in response.

  2. 02

    Follow one explicit engagement.

    Choose Derrida’s discussion of Heidegger in Différance or Deleuze’s preface and Note on Heidegger. Identify what the later text takes up, changes, and refuses.

  3. 03

    Test the claim of influence.

    Mark your evidence as direct citation, interpretive comparison, or a historical hypothesis still needing support. Do not treat a shared word as proof of a shared concept.

  4. 04

    Return to one situation.

    Choose a classification used in a classroom, workplace, or Atlas grammar. Ask whether its categories presuppose fixed identities or emerge through relations. This is a BeyngLab experiment; it does not make those philosophical projects interchangeable.
