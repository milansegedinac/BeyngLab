T13 / TOPOI

An Ereignis reading path / GA 16 and GA 77

Release the grip. Keep the question open.

How can thinking let go of mastery without abandoning responsibility?

Read Gelassenheit through the relation to technical things, waiting, and the open-region. Then ask what releasement makes possible—and what a change of attitude cannot accomplish on its own.

01 / Primary passages

Technical things, waiting, and a revised dialogue.

The 1955 address and the conversations composed in 1944–45 are distinct texts. A revised excerpt of the first conversation appeared with the address in Gelassenheit (1959). The complete conversations later appeared in GA 77. English page numbers below belong to the named translation.

  1. 01 / GA 16 · Memorial Address (1955)

    A yes and a no to technical things

    The address proposes using technical devices while refusing to let them determine everything. Releasement toward things belongs with openness to mystery: a relation to what shows itself while remaining concealed.

    Open the Memorial Address / Discourse on Thinking

    Reading target: Discourse on Thinking, pp. 54–55. Dreyfus reproduces and discusses these passages; the archive entrance links the primary text.

  2. 02 / GA 77: 122–124 · A Triadic Conversation

    Waiting and the open-region

    The interlocutors place thinking in a relation to the open-region that exceeds thinking’s own determination. Their discussion of waiting unsettles the assumption that thinking must first represent and secure its object.

    Country Path Conversations, p. 80
  3. 03 / GA 77: 151–152 · A Triadic Conversation

    Read the revision as evidence

    The conversation speaks of releasement to the worlding of world. The translator’s note records a change to the open-region in the 1959 excerpt. Ask what shifts with that wording before treating the two versions as interchangeable.

    Country Path Conversations, p. 99 / note 58

03 / Distinct readings at Ereignis

Non-willing, shared meaning, material dependence.

BEYOND WILLING

Bret W. Davis

“Another Chorology” reads the conversations as a turn toward releasement. Non-willing exceeds the choice between imposing a result and passively accepting one; the dialogue attempts another relation to the openness in which things appear.

Read Davis at Ereignis
A FREE RELATION

Hubert Dreyfus

“Heidegger on Gaining a Free Relation to Technology” asks what follows once efficiency loses its claim to govern every purpose. Dreyfus also argues that releasement alone does not provide the shared practices and meaningful differences a community needs.

Read Dreyfus at Ereignis
LABOR AND CAPITAL

Patricia Glazebrook

“Heidegger and Economics” brings technological dependence into the relations of capital and labor. Devices can redirect work toward their purchase, operation, and maintenance. Her account puts economic conditions beside the promise of a freer relation to technology.

Read Glazebrook at Ereignis

Another route opens through Reiner Schürmann’s “Heidegger and the Mystical Tradition”. Follow its treatment of releasement and thinking as a situated interpretation; comparative inquiry requires more than identifying a shared vocabulary.

04 / Interpretive problem

What can a freer relation change?

Davis clarifies the challenge to willing; Dreyfus asks what can sustain shared meaning; Glazebrook brings material dependence into the picture. BeyngLab juxtaposes these arguments as a problem for reading and practice, without claiming a direct three-way debate.

A worker required to remain available, a caregiver using essential equipment, and a person choosing a leisure device face different possibilities of refusal. Ask which conditions permit a yes and a no, and which must change before that choice becomes meaningful. These are BeyngLab’s extensions of the reading.

RiskCounter-practice
Release becomes resignationName the obligation that remains and the concrete harm that calls for action.
Releasement becomes a productivity techniqueAsk whether openness can unsettle the goal itself, rather than merely improve its execution.
Withdrawal looks equally available to everyoneIdentify who can refuse, who bears the cost, and whose labor makes the pause possible.
Comparison turns into cultural essenceDistinguish particular texts and practices. Do not assign serenity, surrender, or mastery to entire traditions or regions.

05 / Reading move

A yes, a no, and an unanswered question.

Experimental This exercise tests an interpretation of the address. It does not claim to produce Gelassenheit on demand.

  1. 01

    Anchor the distinction.

    Read the address beside Dreyfus’s discussion. Record what receives a yes, what receives a no, and how openness enters the argument.

  2. 02

    Examine one arrangement.

    Choose a notification system, a scheduling practice, or a familiar device. Describe what it enables and what it asks you and others to make continuously available.

  3. 03

    Keep responsibility in the account.

    Identify one demand you could question, one obligation you must sustain, and a person whose experience might change your description. Compare your proposal with Glazebrook’s account of dependence.

  4. 04

    Retain what remains unresolved.

    Return to the dialogue’s waiting. Write the question that your proposed change cannot settle. Leave space for another answer and revise after listening.
