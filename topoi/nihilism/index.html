T14 / TOPOI

An Ereignis reading path

What still binds? When values lose their force.

Does making new values overcome nihilism—or continue it?

Follow Heidegger’s reading of Nietzsche from devaluation toward the forgottenness of being. Keep Nietzsche’s claims, Heidegger’s interpretation, and our own diagnoses of the present distinct.

01 / Archive trail

Devaluation and forgottenness.

Both passages come from Heidegger’s essay on Nietzsche’s “God is dead,” collected in GA 5, but use different English translations. Their page numbers are not interchangeable. These are Heidegger’s interpretations of Nietzsche, not direct excerpts from Nietzsche’s published books.

  1. 01 / GA 5 · Nietzsche’s Word “God Is Dead”

    The ambiguity of nihilism

    Heidegger reads Nietzsche’s devaluation alongside a revaluation that establishes new values. The world remains even when its previous highest values lose their authority. This complicates a simple story of decline.

    Off the Beaten Track, p. 167
  2. 02 / GA 5 · The Word of Nietzsche: “God Is Dead”

    What happens to being?

    Heidegger shifts the question from the standing of values to being’s withdrawal and its remaining unthought. Metaphysics itself enters the diagnosis. Read this shift carefully before adopting “nihilism” as a label for something you dislike.

    The Question Concerning Technology and Other Essays, p. 110

02 / Terms and distinctions

Three claims to keep apart.

ClaimQuestion
DevaluationHow do formerly binding values lose their force? Trace a process rather than assuming a sudden absence of belief.
RevaluationWhat changes when new values take the place of old ones? Does value-setting itself remain unquestioned?
Forgottenness of beingHeidegger changes the register of the diagnosis. What gets overlooked when attention fixes exclusively on beings?

Nihilismus names the diagnosis explored here. Das Nichts, the nothing, poses a related but distinct question: its disclosure does not amount to declaring everything worthless.

03 / Readers at Ereignis

Mattering, transition, political stakes.

WHAT MATTERS

Robert B. Pippin

“Heidegger on Nietzsche on Nihilism” asks how meaning can lose its binding force without simply being argued away. His account of mattering complicates the idea that people can restore significance merely by choosing new beliefs. His notes also flag Heidegger’s heavy reliance on Nietzsche’s posthumous materials.

Read Pippin at Ereignis
A CHANGING ACCOUNT

Andrew J. Mitchell

“Nihilism in Transition” traces changes in Heidegger’s diagnosis, including a later distinction between the destruction of beings and the withdrawal of being. This reading makes it difficult to treat “Heidegger on nihilism” as a single unchanging position.

Read Mitchell at Ereignis
HISTORICAL RESPONSIBILITY

Gregory Fried

Fried’s account of Introduction to Metaphysics connects its critique of nihilism with Heidegger’s political commitments and asks whether the Western tradition holds resources exceeding his confrontation with it.

Read Fried at Ereignis

04 / Disagreement and risk

How far does the diagnosis reach?

Pippin emphasizes mattering, Mitchell follows textual transformations, and Fried presses political stakes. BeyngLab juxtaposes these readings; they do not form a single direct debate. Ask what evidence would support an epochal diagnosis, and which experiences it might erase.

RiskCounter-practice
Nihilism becomes an insultIdentify a specific claim, practice, or historical change and explain why the term helps.
One history explains everyoneKeep the diagnosis situated. Regional disclosure grammars do not name fixed cultural essences or stages on one universal ladder.
A promised renewal excuses dominationAsk who authorizes the new values and who bears their cost. Philosophical ambition does not settle political responsibility.

05 / Reading move

Test a diagnosis before prescribing renewal.

Experimental A BeyngLab exercise, open to correction.

  1. 01

    Read the change of register.

    Compare the two primary passages. State the difference between devaluation and forgottenness of being, with edition and page references.

  2. 02

    Choose one contested purpose.

    Consider an institution or practice whose declared purpose no longer seems to guide its activity. Record both its declaration and one concrete example. Do not presume that everyone experiences the same loss.

  3. 03

    Preserve the counterexample.

    Find someone for whom that practice still matters. Ask whether a change of values would answer the problem—or merely rename it. Keep the objection beside your diagnosis.
