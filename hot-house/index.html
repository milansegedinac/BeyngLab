04 / VOICES

Hot House Heidis

Read with living voices.

This reading room brings scholars, essayists, and makers into a shared conversation. Each entry starts with a particular work, then asks what its author does with a Heideggerian question. The connections differ; these people need not agree with Heidegger, one another, or BeyngLab.

01 / ART & TECHNOLOGY · BOOK

Iain Thomson

Heidegger, Art, and Postmodernity (2011) asks how art can change a historical understanding of being. Bring the book into conversation with the middle Turn’s danger and the late Turn’s question of dwelling.

Reading question When does a work open a possibility, and when do we project our desired history onto it?

02 / POETRY & CRITIQUE · BOOK

Babette Babich

Words in Blood, Like Flowers (2007) moves among philosophy, poetry, music, Hölderlin, Nietzsche, and Heidegger. Her work gives the Topoi a way to test their own habits of philosophical reading.

Reading question What changes when rhythm or material expression resists our neat concept map?

03 / PUBLIC PHILOSOPHY · VIDEO

Shaggy-Haired Angel

The channel’s Heidegger video essays include Should We CANCEL Martin Heidegger? and Horror Within Heidegger’s Philosophy. Their public-facing form puts difficult thought, attraction, and political judgment in the same viewing space.

Reading question What can a video essay make newly thinkable, and what does its speed or drama leave out?

04 / EVERYDAYNESS · AUDIO & VIDEO

Simon Critchley

Apply-Degger takes a slow, episode-by-episode route through Being and Time, including world, being-with, care, death, and politics. His wider writing carries philosophical questions into public life.

Reading question What happens when an analytic of everyday life becomes an invitation to apply a text to one’s own life?

Official site ↗Listen and watch Apply-Degger ↗
05 / HEIDEGGER & WITTGENSTEIN · BOOK & VIDEO

Lee Braver

Groundless Grounds (2012) puts Wittgenstein and Heidegger into conversation; Heidegger: Thinking of Being (2014) offers another entrance. His Ergo video courses range more widely across philosophy, so the videos and Heidegger scholarship serve different reading tasks.

Reading question Can the ordinary support thought without turning into a foundation that claims to settle it?

06 / HERMENEUTICS & LAW · WISEACRE

Doug Wise / Wiseacre

Wise’s essays at Pete Ferreira’s Ereignis move among Heidegger, affect, technics, biology, and legal interpretation. Two hermeneutical motives in Reading the Constitution makes jurisprudence a concrete site for asking how forms of interpretation order what can count.

Reading question What has a legal frame already decided before a disputed case comes into view?

Thinking with / moving elsewhere

Four distinct departures.

This is a reading path across works, not a school or a measure of Heideggerian fidelity. Each thinker changes a different part of the question; Bogost’s route is looser and often runs through object-oriented ontology and design.

07 / CALL · TEST

Heidegger … Ronell

Avital Ronell

The Telephone Book (1989) reads Heidegger’s call through telephonic technology and drives the question into politics, mediation, interruption, and addiction. The Test Drive pursues her separate inquiry into the regimes that make truth answer to a test.

Reading question Who or what calls us before we choose to answer, and what does a test demand before it tells us what is true?

The Telephone Book · Nebraska Press ↗The Test Drive · Illinois Press ↗
08 / ATMOSPHERE · PRACTICE

Heidegger … Sloterdijk

Peter Sloterdijk

Rules for the Human Zoo replies directly to Heidegger’s Letter on Humanism. The Spheres project pursues shared dwelling through interiors, air, envelopes, and immunitary space. That spatial and anthropotechnical project follows its own course beyond Heidegger scholarship.

Reading question Who makes a world breathable, and who is kept outside its protective envelope?

Rules for the Human Zoo · Suhrkamp ↗Bubbles, Spheres I · MIT Press ↗
09 / TOOL · WITHDRAWAL

Heidegger … Harman

Graham Harman

Tool-Being (2002) makes Heidegger’s tool analysis the hinge of an object metaphysics: objects withdraw from practical use and theory, and from relations with other objects. This is a forceful revision, not an exposition. Harman’s later quadruple object is a different construction from Heidegger’s earth–sky–mortals–divinities.

Reading question What becomes visible when withdrawal belongs to all objects, and what happens to world, history, and mortality in that move?

Tool-Being · book record ↗Test Thing and World in Sixfold ↗
10 / GAME · CARPENTRY

Heidegger … Bogost

Ian Bogost

Alien Phenomenology (2012) makes object-oriented thought operative through ontography, metaphorism, and carpentry; Bogost’s account of Cow Clicker directly applies enframing to social games. He works near Harman while defining his own methods. Heidegger is one interlocutor in a wider practice of games, software, media, and designed artifacts.

Reading question What can a made artifact reveal about a system’s way of ordering things that a conceptual map might miss?

EDITORIAL CHARTER

Hot House entries remain invitations to read, watch, and argue. A linked work starts a particular encounter; it does not imply that its author endorses BeyngLab. The ellipsis marks a question carried between thinkers, not membership in a single Heideggerian school.

Provenance

Historical Work and series titles link to publisher pages, creators’ sites, universities, and the Ereignis archive. Sloterdijk’s Rules names Heidegger’s Letter as its addressee; Bogost names enframing in his Cow Clicker account. Interpretive The ellipses, reading questions, and relative strength of these connections are BeyngLab’s editorial judgments. Ronell’s Test Drive, Sloterdijk’s Spheres, Harman’s object metaphysics, and Bogost’s games each develop projects of their own.
