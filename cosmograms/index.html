C1 / MAKE

Cosmograms / pop fourfolds / EPS

Sixfold your fourfold.

Make a small map of the scenes that orient you. Pick one real thing. Turn it through earth, sky, mortals, and divinities; then ask what the thing gathers and which worlds let that gathering happen. You can start with a single thing and return to your four scenes later.

Three related moves

Begin in a life. Test it in a world.

01 / ULMER · EPS

Pop fourfold

Recall a scene from each of four fields: family; community/history; popular culture; school/career. An Existential Positioning System asks how institutions, memories, hopes, and attractions orient a life. Your fields can change: street, religion, sport, or another scene may matter more.

02 / HEIDEGGER · FOURFOLD

Gathering

Read a particular thing through earth, sky, mortals, and divinities. These are related dimensions of dwelling, not four cultural bins or four boxes that correspond to Ulmer’s fields. “Divinities” remains a question about what calls and withdraws; no belief is required.

03 / BEYNGLAB · SIXFOLD

Thing ↔ World

Make the thing and its world explicit. What gathers here? What wider histories, institutions, ecologies, and platforms make this gathering possible or impossible? Tilt the map with another person’s account or a material constraint, then revise it.

Experimental This crossing is a BeyngLab exercise. Heidegger did not name a sixfold, and Ulmer’s EPS is not a translation of Heidegger’s fourfold. Read the Thing / Fourfold Topos and its archive passages.

QUICK START / ONE ORDINARY THING

A table can open the exercise.

Start with an actual table you know. Give it a place and a history before you give it a theory. You can do this in ten minutes, with a pen or with the form below.

Four scenes. A family meal; a community meeting; a table in a film; a desk at work. One scene or image from each field is enough.

Fourfold. Earth: wood, food, hands, repair. Sky: light, seasons, time of day. Mortals: who eats, works, cleans, or is absent. Divinities / Other(s): hospitality, memory, faith, obligation, or a hope that exceeds the plan.

Sixfold. Thing: what this table actually gathers and where it fails. World: housing, supply chains, institutions, customs, and other tables in other lives. Move a chair or invite someone who has not spoken. What changes?

Your own / 10–20 minutes

Make, turn, and test.

No right answer and no score. Short phrases work. Your entries stay on this device until you download or print them; the site does not collect them.

01 Gather four scenes

Use Ulmer’s popcycle as a starting grid. Recall one object, image, event, or phrase in each field. Leave a field blank if it does not fit; name a better one in your note.

02 Turn one thing through a fourfold

Choose a concrete thing from one of those scenes, or one near you now. Walk around it; touch, listen, or trace its materials where possible. The prompts are lenses, not definitions.

03 Sixfold and put it under pressure

Thing and World make the gathering and its conditions visible. They work across the four terms; they are not two more slots of the same kind.

FROM PERSONAL TO SHARED

Invite a partner to draw the same thing from a different position. Put the two cosmograms next to each other. Keep the mismatch visible: another world may gather a different thing here. Ask who can change the material conditions, who carries the cost, and what your first map made disappear.
