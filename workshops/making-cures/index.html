Breakdown / Sixfold / Making Cures

A gathering. A change. A return.

Choose an ordinary shared situation that no longer works well. Map what sustains it, change one arrangement with the people involved, and return to ask what happened.

Experimental A StudioLab/BeyngLab workshop for collaborative inquiry. Work with an everyday situation or a fictional case; personal disclosure is optional. This exercise does not diagnose or treat illness.

Begin with something small.

Allow 45–60 minutes for the first meeting and agree a later return. Bring paper or use the Sixfold worksheet. A shared table, a confusing meeting, or a room nobody feels able to use gives the group something concrete to examine.

Example: a team’s weekly meeting produces fast decisions, but several members cannot contribute. Do not begin by diagnosing their confidence. Ask how timing, furniture, language, agendas, and expectations shape participation.

Five moves, with room to pause.

  1. 01

    Hear the trouble.

    Each person may offer an observation, a different account, or a pass. Write what happened separately from explanations of why. Identify who is missing and how their account might change the picture.

  2. 02

    Sixfold the situation.

    Earth: materials, bodies, energy, upkeep. Sky: rhythms, light, seasons, time. Mortals: participants, limits, absences. Divinities / Other(s): obligations or hopes exceeding the plan. Thing: what gathers here? World: which institutions, histories, and infrastructures condition it? Thing and World work across the fourfold; they are not two extra boxes of the same kind.

  3. 03

    Choose one change together.

    In the meeting example, try circulating questions beforehand, making space for a written response, or changing the time. Choose with participants. Name who will carry the extra work. An agreed pause or removal of an obligation can also be the intervention.

  4. 04

    Make a shared form.

    Create a short invitation, annotated cosmogram, rearranged space, small performance, or revised practice. Agree who may see or reuse it. Let the form carry disagreements rather than forcing one story of success.

  5. 05

    Return, revise, or stop.

    After trying it, ask what became possible, who carried a new cost, and what remained unresolved. Let the people involved decide whether to continue. A count or score can inform the discussion; it cannot replace their accounts.

Keep a short return note.

RecordPrompt
SituationWhat happened? Whose accounts differ?
GatheringWhat did Sixfold reveal or miss?
ChangeWhat did we agree to try, and who does the work?
ReturnWhen will we meet again? What would lead us to revise or stop?

Keep the note with your group. The workshop has no submission form. The Sixfold worksheet offers its existing download and print options.

Read Wounded research to question your investment in the outcome, and Performance to question demands for improvement. Making cures supplements talking and taking cures through creative doing-with; its success cannot be assumed in advance.
