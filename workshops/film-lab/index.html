W2 / FILM LAB

Road, Voice, Death / 35 minutes

One film. Two readings.

Choose one hilltop return in Abbas Kiarostami’s The Wind Will Carry Us (1999). A visiting filmmaker climbs for a mobile signal near a worker we hear but do not see. Describe that scene, let two Atlas grammars change the question, then keep the objections.

Run the exercise ↘

The shared situation

A call above the village.

A crew has come from Tehran to a Kurdish village to document an anticipated funeral. The woman remains alive. The phone pulls the visitor uphill for reception; nearby, a digger works out of sight. The film’s camera and the crew’s proposed recording sit inside the story’s question about whose life becomes an image.[1][2]

Before reading: Watch one such return in a copy of the film you can access. Editions may differ, so record your own time mark. If you only have the published descriptions linked below, mark your notes “source-based rehearsal”; do not claim to have analyzed shots you have not seen.

Two questions / one scene

What changes with the lens?

These are BeyngLab comparisons, not accounts of Kiarostami’s stated influences.

A1 / IMAGINAL

What does the unseen make active?

The digger’s voice matters before his body appears. The woman’s life organizes the visitors’ waiting while remaining outside their frame. Ask what the film makes perceptible through hearing, delay, and withholding.

Its objection

An unseen person is not evidence of Corbin’s ontological imaginal. Akbar warns that our imagined access to others’ lives can repeat the crew’s presumption.[3]

Read the Imaginal film resonance ↗
A5 / PHARMACOLOGICAL

What does the support arrange?

The phone connects the visitor to work elsewhere and sends him uphill. The planned camera work makes a death into a deadline. Ask how these supports coordinate attention, preserve something, and divert care.[4]

Its objection

The phone does not create the crew’s purpose. Brody also reads communication and cinema as possibilities for contact and freedom, complicating a simple account of technical harm.[2]

Read the Pharmacological encounter ↗

Experimental A provisional joint claim: the film lets a remote deadline, local labor, and the woman’s living time interrupt one another. One lens attends to what remains beyond the image; the other to the tools and institutions directing attention. Test both claims against the scene and against someone else’s account.

W2 / RUNNABLE EXERCISE

Make the second reading answer the first.

35 minutes · 1–6 people

Bring access to the film, or use the linked sources as a clearly labeled rehearsal. Use paper or the local worksheet. A facilitator can read the prompts aloud.

Scope

These are reading questions, not a claim that Kiarostami followed Corbin or Stiegler. A grammar belongs to a revisable interpretation; it cannot be assigned to a people or region.

  1. 05′ / WATCH

    Choose one return.

    Play a hilltop phone-call passage. Note the edition and time mark. If the film is unavailable, read the linked descriptions and label the exercise provisional.

  2. 05′ / DESCRIBE

    Keep form ahead of theory.

    Record the camera’s position, sound, visible work, phone movement, and what you can only infer. Separate observed detail from a critic’s account.

  3. 06′ / IMAGINAL

    Ask after the unseen.

    What does a voice, delay, or absence make active in the scene? Where would this reading pretend to know a person it cannot see?

  4. 06′ / PHARMACOLOGICAL

    Follow the supports.

    What does phone or camera enable, and what demand organizes its use? Include the digger’s labor and the crew’s assignment, then name one possible benefit the critique might miss.

  5. 06′ / OBJECT

    Swap counter-readings.

    Have someone challenge each lens. Which villager’s account is unavailable? Which part of the film refuses the opposition between good and bad technology?

  6. 07′ / REVISE

    Leave a trace of disagreement.

    Write one claim that uses both lenses, cite one scene detail, preserve both objections, and state what further viewing or testimony would change your mind.

Evidence and status

Read beside the film.

  1. Primary
    Janus Films, The Wind Will Carry Us (1999), distributor record

    Premise and unseen figures; the film itself is the primary work for scene observations.

  2. Interpretive
    Richard Brody, “DVD of the Week: The Wind Will Carry Us,” The New Yorker, 2010

    Hilltop phone and digger, with an emphasis on the openings of communication and cinema.

  3. Interpretive
    Kaveh Akbar, “The Wind Will Carry Us: Dust to Dust,” The Criterion Collection, 2025

    Offscreen lives, labor, and the ethics of the visitor’s and viewer’s gaze.

  4. Primary
    Bernard Stiegler, “Relational Ecology and the Digital Pharmakon,” trans. Patrick Crogan, Culture Machine 13 (2012)

    Technics, external memory, attention, and care. Applying this text to the film is BeyngLab’s experiment.

  5. Primary
    Henry Corbin, “Mundus Imaginalis,” in Swedenborg and Esoteric Islam, trans. Leonard Fox

    Corbin’s ontological imaginal cannot be inferred from an offscreen device in cinema.

Experimental The two-lens pairing, timing, questions, and provisional joint claim belong to BeyngLab. They are open to correction.
